Why Montreal Agencies Charge $3,000 a Month for GEO, and What $900 Actually Buys You

By Nick Aren, Solutions Marketing VIP - Published October 2026

Most Montreal agencies start their GEO retainers at $3,000 a month, and I decided to close that gap instead of complaining about it. In this article I will explain what GEO and CRO actually mean, in language your grandmother would understand, and why I price my versions at $900 and $1,500 instead of agency rates. No acronym soup, no jargon. If you can use email, you can follow this.

First, why agencies charge so much

I researched the market before setting my prices. Here is what I found. Established GEO agencies in North America typically start at $3,000 a month. Mid-range packages run $4,000 to $7,000. One-off GEO audits alone often cost $1,000 to $4,000, before any monthly work even begins. CRO audits from established consultancies run $2,500 to $4,500 as a single payment, with monthly programs starting at $5,000.

Are these agencies scamming people? Mostly, no. Their costs are real. They employ teams, they run expensive tracking software, they serve enterprise clients with hundreds of pages, and their sales process involves meetings about meetings. Somebody has to pay for all that, and that somebody is the client.

But here is the part that bothered me. A plumber in Lasalle, a salon in Ahuntsic, a landscaping company on the South Shore: none of them have $3,000 a month for GEO. Nobody is serving them. The agencies chase the enterprise contracts, the small business owners get told the table starts at three thousand, and they quietly walk away with nothing. That is the gap I decided to close.

The gap, in one paragraph: real GEO and CRO work costs $3,000 and up per month from agencies, small businesses cannot pay that, and almost nobody serves the space between DIY guessing games and agency rates. I built my services to sit exactly in that empty space. Not the cheapest, because junk lives down there. Not agency-priced, because that excludes the businesses I actually want to help.

OK, so what is GEO? Explained like you are my uncle

You know how you used to Google things? Ten blue links, you pick one, you read it. That is old search.

The new way people search: they open ChatGPT, or Gemini, or Perplexity, and they just ask a question in normal words. "Who is the best plumber near me?" "What should a website audit cost in Montreal?" And instead of giving ten links, the AI answers in a paragraph and mentions a few businesses by name.

Now the obvious question: when the AI writes that answer, where do the business names come from? The AI reads websites, gathers facts, and decides which businesses to mention based on what it finds. GEO, which stands for Generative Engine Optimization, is the work of making sure the AI knows who you are, what you sell, what you charge, and why people trust you.

Think of the AI as a librarian who has read the entire internet but has a terrible memory for details. If your website is written in a confusing way, the librarian shrugs and mentions your competitor instead. GEO is handing that librarian a clean, organized card about your business: here is the name, here is what we do, here are the prices, here are the answers to the twenty questions customers actually ask.

Concretely, my GEO Readiness Audit at $900 does four things:

  • The card for the librarian. A file called llms.txt sits at the root of your website, written so AI tools can read it instantly and know exactly who you are, what you offer, and what your prices are.
  • Labels on your shelves. Your pages get small invisible labels, called schema markup, that tell machines what each thing is. This one is a local business. This one is a question and its answer. This one is a person and their job title. Machines stop guessing.
  • Straight answers at the top. Your key pages get rewritten so the first sentence delivers the conclusion. The AI, like your busy customers, rarely reads past the first paragraph.
  • A visibility report. We ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude real questions about your industry in your area, and we record whether you show up, which competitors do, and why. That is your starting line. You cannot improve what you have never measured.

You get a report showing all of it, in plain English and French, plus a score you can track over time. And once the foundation is built, my monthly GEO monitoring at $850 re-runs the same questions every month, updates your card when your business changes, and adds one new question-and-answer article per month. Up or down, you see the trend.

And what is CRO? Same energy, different problem

GEO gets people to your door. CRO is what happens once they are inside.

CRO stands for Conversion Rate Optimization. The conversion rate is simply the percentage of visitors who do the thing you want: call you, fill the form, book the appointment. If 100 people visit your site this month and 2 of them contact you, your conversion rate is 2 percent.

Here is the part that surprises everyone. Most websites are actually pretty good at getting found and terrible at closing. People arrive, look around for three seconds, and leave. Not because the business is bad, but because the website has friction: slow pages, a form asking for their blood type, no phone number visible, no reviews, no reason to trust you, buttons too small to tap with a thumb.

The store analogy, because it works. Your website is your storefront. CRO is the retail expert who walks through your store like a customer and notices everything wrong: the lights take ten seconds to turn on, the price tags are upside down, the checkout counter is hidden in the back, the door sticks. You, standing behind the counter every day, stopped noticing all of it years ago.

My CRO Audit at $1,500 is exactly that walkthrough, on mobile and desktop:

  • The first screen people see, judged honestly: do they know what you do, why to trust you, and what to do next within two seconds?
  • Speed and friction: load times, broken images, forms asking for too much
  • Trust signals: reviews, testimonials, guarantees, real photos, and where each one belongs
  • A real thumb-through on a phone: can you tap the number, does the form work, are the buttons big enough?

The deliverable is a numbered fix list, ranked by effort versus impact, written in language you could forward directly to your developer. No A/B testing theater, no dashboards you will never open, just the list of things blocking sales, in order.

A word of honesty about the cheap end of the market: you will find $27 CRO reports and $99 GEO packages online. They are generated by software spitting out generic warnings, the same report for a bakery and a law firm. The expensive end is real work at enterprise prices. The middle, the honest, human, small business middle, was empty. That is the corner I decided to stand in.

Why I am the one closing the gap

Two reasons, and you can check both.

First, I did it to myself before doing it for anyone. My own site went through the full GEO treatment: the llms.txt file, the schema labels, the answer-first rewrites, the whole thing. It is public, you can look at it right now. I do not sell anything I have not already survived.

Second, my costs are simply lower. No office downtown, no account managers, no sales team, no enterprise software licenses padded into the invoice. It is me, my methodology, and smart tools that keep my hours down. When your overhead is close to zero, you can charge $900 for work agencies bill $3,000, still make a fair margin, and sleep fine. The agencies are not evil. They are just big, and big is expensive.

How to know if any of this is for you: ask ChatGPT right now, "who does SEO audits in Montreal," and see if you appear. Ask yourself when a visitor last contacted you through your website. If both answers disappoint you, one of these two services is probably your next move. And if you are not sure which one, tell me what you found and I will tell you honestly, including if the answer is neither.

The gap is closed. Your move.

Pensez à l'IA comme à une bibliothécaire qui a lu tout Internet mais qui a une mémoire épouvantable des détails. Et c'est quoi le CRO? Même énergie, problème différent

Le GEO amène les gens à votre porte. Le CRO, c'est ce qui se passe une fois qu'ils sont entrés.

CRO signifie optimisation du taux de conversion. Le taux de conversion, c'est simplement le pourcentage de visiteurs qui font ce que vous voulez : vous appeler, remplir le formulaire, prendre le rendez-vous. Si 100 personnes visitent votre site ce mois-ci et que 2 d'entre elles vous contactent, votre taux de conversion est de 2 pour cent.

Voici la partie qui surprend tout le monde. La plupart des sites web sont plutôt bons pour se faire trouver et épouvantables pour conclure. Les gens arrivent, regardent pendant trois secondes, et partent. Pas parce que l'entreprise est mauvaise, mais parce que le site web crée de la friction : des pages lentes, un formulaire qui demande leur groupe sanguin, aucun numéro de téléphone visible, aucun avis, aucune raison de vous faire confiance, des boutons trop petits pour être tapés au pouce.

L'analogie du magasin, parce qu'elle fonctionne. Votre site web, c'est votre commerce. Le CRO, c'est l'expert du commerce de détail qui traverse votre magasin comme un client et remarque tout ce qui cloche : les lumières prennent dix secondes à s'allumer, les étiquettes de prix sont à l'envers, la caisse est cachée au fond, la porte colle.
